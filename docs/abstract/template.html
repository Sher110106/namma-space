Namma Space

Technical abstract · Techfest IIT Bombay, Round 1 · Team ID: ${team_id_html}

Namma Space turns one ordinary phone video into a level 3D scene of an indoor space, with an estimated real-world scale, that anyone can walk through in a web browser. It needs one command, no LiDAR and no hand editing. Every tool is open and allows commercial use.

What a visitor can do

Walk in first person with the keyboard or a phone touch pad. Walls and furniture stop you (${walls_n} random walks and ${pushes_n} pushes into walls: ${penetrations} positions inside blocked cells). Jump with the minimap. Press C to compare a held-out photo with the render at the same pose. Press T for a fixed tour that prints the machine's own frame rate. Press K to measure between two points, with the scale interval shown. The scene downloads ${dev_mb} (SD file ${sd_mb} MB plus a ${preview_mb} MB preview that loads before it).

1. Capture SOP

The SOP is a one-page phone card (docs/SOP_card.pdf) and a longer page that gives the reason for each rule (docs/SOP.md). One phone, one lens, about 20 minutes of filming. The sensor is the phone camera only, by choice.

Settings

SettingValue
Video4K, 30 fps (25 fps if lights flicker), HDR off, 1x lens, landscape
Focus, exposureLocked (AE/AF LOCK) on a mid-bright wall
StabilisationEnhanced stabilisation, Action mode, Auto FPS off
RoomNobody in shot, fans off, door wedged open, mirrors covered, all lights on
Before filmingClean lens, 5 GB free, a 30 s test clip run through ./ns check

Walk (take A, 3 to 4 min, one continuous video)

  • Hold still 3 s in the doorway.
  • Outer loop: 0.5 m from the walls, camera across the room.
  • Inner loop: 1.5 m from the walls, camera at the nearest wall, tilted slightly down. Tilt up to each ceiling corner for 2 s.
  • Low pass: waist height, 30° down, around tables and furniture.
  • Take B (optional): cross the door slowly both ways to link a second space.
  • Never: spin on the spot, zoom, switch lens, walk fast.

2. Pipeline and approach

Figure 2. The pipeline, with stage times measured in this run (${scene_title}). One command, ./ns run video.mp4, runs it; each stage writes a record and can resume.

Poses: a race between variants

COLMAP 4.2.1 runs ${sfm_n_variants} pose variants (SIFT or ALIKED features, LightGlue matching, sequential matching with loop detection). A rule picks the winner: the highest share of frames in one model at low reprojection error. Here variant ${sfm_name} won (${sfm_desc}): ${sfm_reg} of frames registered, ${sfm_reproj} mean reprojection error. Selection note: ${sfm_why}.

Level and scale

The camera path gives the up direction and the floor. The scale comes from cues with a stated uncertainty. In this run the cue is ${scale_cue}, giving ${scale_interval}; the scale is ${scale_status}. Up-direction cues agree to ${up_resid} (gate: 5°). The fused scale and its interval travel with the scene, so a later tape measurement rescales it without retraining.

Training

gsplat 1.5.3 with the MCMC strategy, a bilateral grid for exposure drift and anti-aliasing. We checked that our trainer matches the published one on Mip-NeRF 360 “room”: ${mip_psnr} dB against ${mip_pub} dB published (${mip_delta} dB). Two variants train: r0, and r1 (r0 plus a depth loss). This run shipped ${train_name} (${train_why}): ${n_gs} splats, PSNR ${psnr} dB on ${heldout_n} held-out photos.

Splat for looks, grid for walking

Visitors see the splats. Collision, the minimap and routes use a light 5 cm grid computed from the same geometry (${walkable} m² walkable here). The splat file alone is a poor collision surface, because it has no clean walls. The browser never loads a mesh.

Export and viewer

Cleaning removes floaters and splats outside the room. Export writes SPZ files: an SD scene (${sd_count} splats, ${sd_mb} MB, PSNR change ${sd_psnr_drop} dB from cleaning and compression) and a ${preview_mb} MB preview that loads before it. The viewer is static files (three.js and Spark). It starts at a pixel ratio that fits the GPU and lowers it if the frame rate drops.

Failure handling

Every stage has an exit code and a record. A capture check gates the run. A quality gate flags weak results instead of hiding them. Gate warnings this run:

Bottlenecks and compute request

Training (${t_train}) and the pose race (${t_sfm}) take almost all the time. Both run on one shared GPU, so other jobs stretch them. Peak GPU memory in training was ${peak_gpu} GB, so memory is not the limit; time is. A GPU with no other jobs, or a second one, would let the training variants run side by side instead of in turn.

Limits

  • Our fidelity numbers come from public rooms and this run, not from every room. The public-room checks do not show that every capture will work.
  • Scale is an estimate with an interval. It is ${scale_status}.
  • Phone-only geometry scored below iPhone LiDAR fusion on both public rooms (Section 3).
  • Viewer numbers come from one Apple M1 in headless Chrome. Other hardware, including integrated graphics, is unverified.
  • Ceilings and close-up walls look soft. Glass, mirrors and bare walls are hard for the pose race (see the SOP).
  • Training at 1920 px frames has not been timed on a full run.

Roadmap, not built

Multiple floors; WebXR AR on phones; registration of an architect's plan. Search, jump, routes and follow are built as a Round 2 preview over hand-placed places; Round 1 does not depend on them.
